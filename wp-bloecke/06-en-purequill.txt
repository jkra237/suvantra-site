    <p class="augbraue">New in 1.5.0</p>
    <h2>Even closer to Word.</h2>
    <p class="lead">The new version catches up where Word used to be ahead: tables, page
       breaks, headers and footers. And every change can be undone.</p>
    <div class="saeulen" style="margin-top:30px">
      <div class="saeule">
        <span class="nr">Tables</span>
        <h3>Tables as in Word</h3>
        <p>Styles in one click, merge and split cells, shading, borders and column width. Long tables repeat their header row on every page.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="saeule">
        <span class="nr">Pages</span>
        <h3>Page for page like Word</h3>
        <p>Word files split into pages as they do in Word: same line heights, tables in the same place, in print too.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="saeule">
        <span class="nr">Headers and footers</span>
        <h3>With their own toolbar</h3>
        <p>You see page number and date while you type; logos, formatting and tab stops come along from Word, .odt and .rtf.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="saeule">
        <span class="nr">Undo</span>
        <h3>Everything can be taken back</h3>
        <p>Undo and redo work for every change, including tables, images, lists and paragraph formatting.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <p class="klein" style="margin-top:30px">Plus an Insert menu as in Word and redesigned notes.
      <a href="/purequill/changes/">Everything that is new</a>.</p>

</section>

    <p class="augbraue">Tools for writers</p>
    <h2>What other word processors do not have.</h2>
    <p class="lead">A panel beside the text shows which words are piling up, and it weighs
       them: a word forty times across a novel is harmless, four times in one paragraph
       is not. A word goal shows how far you have come, and text clean-up tidies away
       double spaces and empty paragraphs.</p>

<section class="wrap">
  <p class="augbraue">Notes</p>
  <h2>Notes right where they belong.</h2>
  <p class="lead">Pin a note to a passage and it sits in the margin beside it — like a
     comment in Word, just for you. Comments from Word files come in as notes.</p>
  <div class="saeulen" style="margin-top:30px">
    <div class="saeule">
      <h3>On the passage</h3>
      <p>One note can be pinned to several passages. They light up as soon as you point at the note.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="saeule">
      <h3>From Word</h3>
      <p>Comments from .docx, .doc and .odt appear as notes in the margin, at their place in the text.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="saeule">
      <h3>Glossary</h3>
      <p>A glossary keeps names and terms beside the text, in its own view.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="flaeche">
  <div class="wrap">
    <p class="augbraue">Autosave</p>
    <h2>Saved while you write.</h2>
    <p class="lead">You do not have to think about saving. Everything you write is saved as you
       go — on your computer, not in a cloud, and without being online.</p>
    <div class="saeulen" style="margin-top:30px">
      <div class="saeule">
        <h3>Continuously</h3>
        <p>About a second after your last keystroke, the text is saved on your computer.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="saeule">
        <h3>Checkpoints</h3>
        <p>Every 30 seconds the program records a checkpoint. The version history reaches back up to a month.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="saeule">
        <h3>Backup file</h3>
        <p>If you like, it also writes the whole state to a file at regular intervals — for example into a folder that Dropbox or OneDrive keeps in sync.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
